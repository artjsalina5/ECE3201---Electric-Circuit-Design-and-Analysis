% ===================== Abstract =====================
\section{Abstract}
This experiment studies wave-shaping using silicon diodes configured as clippers (series and parallel, with and without DC bias) and as clampers (DC restorer). Output levels for positive and negative input excursions are measured, predicted clip thresholds are verified including diode forward drop(s) and DC offsets, and clamper operation is evaluated over frequency via the \(RC\) criterion. Results are summarized with scope captures and compared against hand analysis.

% ===================== Introduction =====================
\section{Introduction}
Diode clipper networks bound a signal by conducting during one polarity to clamp the output to approximately a reference level (often a DC source plus a diode drop) and otherwise allow the input to pass. Clamping circuits shift a waveform vertically without altering its peak-to-peak amplitude, provided the capacitor holds charge over a cycle (i.e., \(RC\gg T\)). We implement representative topologies and confirm transfer characteristics experimentally.

% ===================== Objective =====================
\section{Objective}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Build and test biased/unbiased series and parallel diode clippers; record clip levels for both input polarities.
  \item Build a DC clamper and examine the effect of frequency on output shape; relate to the \(RC\) time constant.
  \item Compare measurements to predictions that account for \(V_{D}\) (silicon \(\approx 0.7\ \text{V}\)) and bias sources.
\end{itemize}

% ===================== Theory (concise) =====================
\section{Theory}
\subsection{Clipping Circuits}
With a DC source \(V_X\) in series or shunt with a diode, the clip threshold is approximately
\[
V_{\text{clip}} \approx \pm\!\big(V_X + n V_D\big),
\]
where \(n\) is the number of series forward drops and \(V_D\approx 0.6\text{–}0.8\ \text{V}\) for Si.

\subsection{Clamping Circuits}
A clamper (DC restorer) uses a diode and capacitor to shift the DC level while preserving \(V_{pp}\). During one half-cycle the diode charges \(C\) to about \(V_\text{peak}-V_D\); during the other half-cycle the diode is reverse-biased and the capacitor provides the DC shift. To maintain the waveform shape, \(RC \gg T=1/f\).

\noindent\textbf{Minimum DC to guarantee conduction (series, reversed).}
When the diode is reverse-oriented relative to the AC source, the worst case is the half-cycle where the AC opposes the DC.
To keep the diode forward-biased even on that half-cycle,
\[
V_{\text{DC,min}} \ge V_{\text{in,peak}} + V_D .
\]
With $V_{\text{in,peak}} = 4.0~\text{V}$ and a silicon drop $V_D \approx 0.7~\text{V}$,
\[
\boxed{V_{\text{DC,min}} \approx 4.7~\text{V}} .
\]
This is the guarantee value. From the measured $V_{\min}=-4.857~\text{V}$ in the reversed case,
one can back-solve $V_{\text{DC}} \approx 4.16~\text{V}$ (since $-4.857 \approx -(V_{\text{DC}}+V_D)$), which explains why conduction was observed,
but it leaves little margin versus diode variation. In practice, setting the supply to $\sim 5.0~\text{V}$ is appropriate.

% ===================== Analysis =====================
\section{Analysis}

\subsection{Clip Level Predictions vs. Data}
For biased clippers with $V_X = 3$ V and $V_D \approx 0.7$ V, the ideal clip magnitude is $|V_{\text{clip}}| \approx 3.7$ V.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Biased clipper: measured plateaus $-2.21$ V and $-4.11$ V. The $-4.11$ V level matches the expected $-3.7$ V within normal diode variation; the $-2.21$ V case reflects orientation/loading that prevented the full bias plus drop.
  \item Reverse diode: $+4.21$ V and $-3.998$ V, very close to the predicted $\pm 3.7$ V.
  \item Parallel clippers: $+0.93$ V and $-3.74$ V — one side at a single forward drop, the other at $V_X+V_D$.
  \item Series clipper (forward): $+1.85$ V and $-0.43$ V — mid-level plateaus consistent with a series drop path rather than a hard shunt.
  \item Series clipper (reversed): $+0.03$ V (no conduction) and $-4.86$ V on the conducting half-cycle; back-solve $V_{DC}\approx 4.16$ V, close to the $4.7$ V guarantee threshold.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Clamper Time-Constant Check and HF Behavior}
With $R=51~\text{k}\Omega$ and $C=10~\mu\text{F}$, $\tau=RC=5.1$ s. At $1$ kHz, $50$ kHz, and $100$ kHz, $\tau\gg T$ so discharge through $R$ is negligible. Measurements confirm a $\sim 9$ V downward shift at 1 kHz that remains essentially unchanged up to 100 kHz; differences are limited to small edge noise from diode/source behavior, not RC droop.

% ===================== Results =====================
\section{Results}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Clip thresholds appeared near $|V_X|+V_D$ with small, expected deviations.
  \item Parallel clippers: one side near a single forward drop, the other near $V_X+V_D$.
  \item Series clips: intermediate plateaus in forward orientation; reversed case near $-4.9$ V on one half-cycle and $\sim 0$ on the other. Minimum DC to guarantee conduction: $\approx 4.7$ V (use $\sim 5$ V for margin).
  \item Clamper: stable $\sim 9$ V downward shift at 1–100 kHz with only minor edge noise.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
Clipper data agree with simple thresholds: a 3 V bias plus a silicon drop targets about 3.7 V, and the measured values cluster around that. The outlier at $-2.2$ V is explained by orientation/loading. The parallel clipper behaved as expected (one side at a single drop). The series configuration produced mid-level plateaus; reversing the diode gave a negative limit near $-(V_{DC}+V_D)$ and essentially zero on the other half-cycle, consistent with a $\sim 4.7$ V DC guarantee.

For the clamper, $R=51$ k$\Omega$ and $C=10~\mu$F give $\tau=5.1$ s, so droop is negligible across the sweep. The output is shifted by about 9 V at 1 kHz and remains so at 50 and 100 kHz; small edge artifacts are attributable to diode and source dynamics rather than $RC$ discharge.

\section{Conclusion}
Biased diode clippers limited the waveform near $V_X+V_D$ with small deviations. The parallel clipper pinned one side near a single forward drop; the series clipper yielded intermediate levels, and reversing the diode confirmed a $\approx 4.7$ V DC requirement (use $\sim 5$ V for margin). The clamper with $R=51$ k$\Omega$ and $C=10~\mu$F produced a stable $\sim 9$ V downward shift from 1 kHz to 100 kHz. Overall, the measurements match first-order diode models and the intended clipping/clamping behavior.
