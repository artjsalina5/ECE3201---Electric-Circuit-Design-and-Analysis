% ===================== Abstract =====================
\section{Abstract}
This experiment studies wave-shaping using silicon diodes configured as clippers (series and parallel, with and without DC bias) and as clampers (DC restorer). You will measure output levels for positive and negative input excursions, verify predicted clip thresholds that include diode forward drop(s) and any DC offset sources, and evaluate clamper operation over frequency via the RC time constant criterion. Results will be summarized with scope captures and compared against hand analysis and optional SPICE simulations.

% ===================== Introduction =====================
\section{Introduction}
Diode clipper networks bound a signal by conducting during one polarity to clamp the output to approximately a reference level (often a DC source plus diode drop) and otherwise allow the input to pass. Clamping circuits shift a waveform vertically without altering its peak-to-peak amplitude, provided the capacitor holds charge over a cycle (i.e., \(RC\gg T\)). We will implement representative topologies and confirm the expected transfer characteristics experimentally.

% ===================== Objective =====================
\section{Objective}
\begin{itemize}

  \item Build a DC clamper and examine the effect of frequency on output shape; relate to the \(RC\) time constant.

% ===================== Theory (concise, mirrored pacing) =====================
\section{Theory}
\subsection{Clipping Circuits}
A single diode with a series resistor forms a basic clipper. With a DC source \(V_X\) in series (or anti-series) with the diode, the output clips near \(\pm (V_X + V_D)\) depending on orientation. For an ideal diode the transition is abrupt; a real diode presents \(V_D \approx \SI{0.6}{\text{to}\,0.8}{V}\) and finite slope.

For biased parallel clippers (diode + source to ground), the output follows input until the diode forward-bias condition is met; then the output is held roughly at the bias reference plus \(V_D\).

\subsection{Clamping Circuits}
A clamper (DC restorer) uses a diode and capacitor to shift the DC level while preserving the input's peak-to-peak. During one half-cycle the diode conducts and charges the capacitor to approximately the input peak (minus \(V_D\)); during the opposite half-cycle the diode is reverse-biased and the capacitor provides the DC shift. To maintain the waveform shape, the discharge through \(R\) over a period should be small: \(RC \gg T=1/f\).

% ===================== Analysis =====================
\section{Analysis}
\subsection{Predicted Clip Levels}
Assume silicon \(V_D\approx\SI{0.7}{V}\) unless measured otherwise. For each topology and polarity, compute the expected threshold \(V_0\) when the diode conducts:
\[ V_{\text{clip}} \approx \pm( V_X + n\,V_D ) \]
where \(V_X\) is the DC bias magnitude (if present) and \(n\) is the number of series forward drops along the conduction path. Compare these predictions to measured \(V_0\).

\subsection{Clamper Time-Constant Check}
With \(R=\SI{2}{k\Omega}\) and \(C=\SI{10}{\micro F}\), \(\tau=RC=\SI{20}{ms}\). For \(f=\SI{1}{kHz}\), \(T=\SI{1}{ms}\) so \(\tau/T\approx 20\). For \(\SI{50}{kHz}\) and \(\SI{100}{kHz}\), \(T=\SI{20}{\micro s}\) and \(\SI{10}{\micro s}\) respectively, yielding \(\tau/T\gg 1\). Note any deviations due to source resistance, diode leakage, and ESR.
